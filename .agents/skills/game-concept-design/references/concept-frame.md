# Concept Frame

## Minimum Brief

- creator intent and the moment worth making;
- target player as a hypothesis;
- player fantasy and recurring player verbs;
- `The game is fun because ...`;
- mechanism, expected behavior, desired experience, observable signal, and
  failure signal;
- two to four design pillars and explicit anti-pillars;
- engagement reasons at applicable timescales;
- `return_reason` and `memory_reason`;
- system-only result and narrative contribution;
- signature moment or interaction hypothesis;
- reason this game exists beyond its genre;
- scope and next unknown.

## Multi-Timescale Check

- seconds: is the immediate interaction satisfying or meaningfully expressive?
- minutes: what decision, tension, puzzle, or objective sustains attention?
- session: what discovery, run, quest, build, or narrative pressure continues?
- hours or campaign: what understanding, attachment, strategy, or progression
  compounds?
- years: what mechanic, place, music, revelation, achievement, decision, or
  social memory might remain?

Mark irrelevant timescales rather than inventing retention mechanics.

## Coherence Checks

Reject or revise a pillar that does not constrain decisions. Treat `great story`,
`cool world`, `lots of content`, and `polished` as incomplete fun explanations.
World claims should ideally become `world fact -> observable trace -> player
inference`. Narrative should add motivation, tension, meaning, attachment,
context, or surprise rather than coexist decoratively.
