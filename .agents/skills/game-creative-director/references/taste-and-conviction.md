# Taste and Conviction

## Taste Is a Contextual Prior

Record what the creator enjoyed, the context, and the decomposed property:
operation, puzzle structure, skill ceiling, progression, party construction,
exploration, discovery, storytelling, atmosphere, collection, optimization,
risk, surprise, or replayability. Do not map a title directly to a preference.

Allow apparently conflicting modes such as `system_first` and `story_first`.
Distinguish an enduring preference from a project-specific desire.

## Conversation Prompts

Choose one or two that can change the next decision:

- What exact moment do you want to experience?
- What part must survive every redesign?
- What could be removed without regret?
- What would make this feel like your game?
- What would make you embarrassed to release it?
- Which existing game most clearly exposes this idea's weakness?
- What would make this a signature work rather than a competent entry?

Do not ask all prompts at once.

## Conviction Dimensions

- `concept_conviction`: belief in the premise before play;
- `prototype_conviction`: desire to continue after touching the prototype;
- `system_conviction`: belief that interactions can sustain the intended play;
- `aesthetic_conviction`: belief in the sensory identity;
- `signature_conviction`: willingness to claim the finished work as distinctly
  the creator's.

Record supporting and opposing evidence for each. For `signature_candidate`,
ask whether the creator would play it, can explain its fun and differentiation,
still wants to make it after criticism, enjoyed the prototype, and would be
proud to release it. A `yes` without rationale is weak evidence.
