# Scientific Discovery Lens

Use this lens to improve the question, not merely to elaborate the proposed
answer.

## Questions

- What is the exact object, claim, scope, and observable consequence?
- Which assumptions are definitions, empirical premises, approximations, or
  inherited conventions?
- Can the problem be reformulated from first principles or in a representation
  where invariants, symmetries, conservation laws, scales, or causal structure
  become clearer?
- What is the simplest nontrivial case, boundary case, degeneracy, or
  counterexample?
- Which known result or simpler mechanism may subsume the proposed insight?
- What evidence would distinguish the claim from its strongest alternative?
- Are correctness, novelty, importance, and tractability being evaluated
  independently?

## Productive Moves

- Replace a vague question with a falsifiable one.
- Search for invariants, dual descriptions, limiting regimes, and changes of
  variables or representation.
- Derive necessary and sufficient conditions separately.
- Attempt disproof before expanding the theory.
- Prefer a minimal calculation, pilot, or decisive experiment before a large
  program of work.
- Treat a null result, counterexample, or non-novel result as useful evidence.

## Output

Report the strongest surviving formulation, evidence status, main objection,
cheapest discriminating test, and what result would justify continuing or
stopping.
