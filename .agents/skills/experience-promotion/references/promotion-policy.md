# Promotion, Maintenance, and Retirement Policy

Use this reference before materially changing durable guidance or enforcement.

## Prepare the Intervention

1. Name the observed problem or verified success path.
2. State the causal confidence and live alternatives.
3. Identify overlap, conflicts, and the canonical target.
4. Compress the intervention and narrow its scope.
5. Compare a prompt rule with a deterministic mechanism.
6. Define expected benefit, collateral cost, validation, monitoring signal, and rollback trigger.

Validation strength should increase with scope, blast radius, and irreversibility. Material global-policy changes, privacy or security boundaries, destructive automation, broad workflow restrictions, and parameter-level adaptations require explicit human review.

## Allowed Operations

- **Add** only when no canonical rule or mechanism covers the need.
- **Merge** overlapping guidance into one clearer canonical statement.
- **Compress** without losing decision-relevant distinctions or exceptions.
- **Demote** guidance whose useful scope is narrower than its current location.
- **Archive** superseded evidence when provenance may matter later.
- **Delete** redundant, disproven, stale, or model-obsolete scaffolding when no audit need remains.

An instruction should not remain merely because it once helped. Periodically ask whether it still changes outcomes, whether a lower-cost mechanism now exists, and whether stronger models or tools made it obsolete.

## Anti-Patterns

- every failure becomes a rule;
- append-only prompt accretion;
- the same knowledge duplicated in instructions, Skills, handoffs, and documentation;
- a global rule overfit to one task;
- process retained after its evidence or environment has changed;
- broader enforcement chosen as a status upgrade;
- monitoring that repeatedly scans unrelated work for confirmation.

## Deployment Record

For a material intervention, record the evidence, confidence, target, expected benefit, possible collateral effects, validation performed, authorization when required, monitoring signal, rollback method, and review date.

Narrow, supersede, archive, or remove the intervention when it blocks valid work, fails to mitigate the problem, conflicts with stronger evidence, rests on a disproven explanation, or becomes stale.
