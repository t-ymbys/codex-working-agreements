# Evidence and Playtests

## Evidence Levels

- `E0`: concept speculation;
- `E1`: analytical reasoning;
- `E2`: runnable prototype;
- `E3`: creator playtest;
- `E4`: internal human playtest;
- `E5`: target-player playtest;
- `E6`: behavioral telemetry.

Do not treat the scale as automatic quality ordering. Each level has different
biases. A creator test is essential for conviction but weak evidence of target
appeal; telemetry shows behavior but not necessarily motive.

## Selective Dimensions

Choose only those relevant to the question: moment-to-moment fun, decision
quality, depth, mastery, agency, difficulty, fairness, feel, pacing, discovery,
surprise, world depth, narrative resonance, visual coherence, audio coherence,
UX clarity, originality, replay value, return reason, memory reason, and
production feasibility.

Each rating or judgment needs evidence level, confidence, rationale, provenance,
version, and limitations. Pairwise comparisons often yield stronger decisions
than absolute numbers.

## Human Observation

Capture what happened before asking why: repeated actions, pauses, confusion,
experiments, voluntary retries, revisits, optimization, exploration, quitting,
unused mechanics, unexpected strategies, and spontaneous discussion. Avoid
coaching unless the protocol tests onboarding recovery.

Ask creators separately: Did I actually enjoy this? Did I want another run? Did
the imagined fun occur? What felt fake or forced? What surprised me positively?

## Adversarial Review

At a milestone ask:

- Why is this actually mediocre?
- If released among hundreds of competent indie games, why remember it?
- Why choose it from the same shelf as the creator's favorites?
- What might still matter in ten years?
- Why play again, or why is replay intentionally unnecessary?
