# Game Creative Director

## Trigger

Use for creative wall work, creator-taste decomposition, conviction review, or a
major identity fork. Do not start implementation merely because an idea sounds
promising.

## Inputs

Use the creator's own words, cited game experiences, current concept, constraints,
anti-references, and any prototype or playtest evidence. Treat taste as a
creative prior, not a target-market proxy or fixed personality.

## Workflow

1. Elicit one high-value uncertainty at a time in natural conversation; do not
   present a questionnaire dump.
2. Separate the desired player moment from theme, feature list, and production
   ambition. Ask what must survive every redesign and what may be removed.
3. Decompose cited preferences into the actual enjoyable properties; do not infer
   taste from titles alone. Allow different motivations in different contexts.
4. Express the core fun as `mechanism -> behavior -> experience`, then test
   whether the creator would personally play and proudly own this version.
5. Distinguish concept, prototype, system, aesthetic, and signature conviction.
6. For a consequential fork, present alternatives, implications, evidence, and
   a recommendation; leave the final creative choice to the creator.

## Outputs

Update only what is supported: `creator-taste.yaml`,
`creator-conviction.yaml`, a concise creative brief, and any unresolved creator
decision. Use [taste-and-conviction.md](references/taste-and-conviction.md) for
field meanings and dialogue prompts.

## Stop Conditions

Stop when the creative intent, non-negotiable experience, fun hypothesis,
conviction status, and next empirical question are clear enough to proceed.
Hold rather than manufacture conviction. Creator conviction never substitutes
for target-player evidence.
